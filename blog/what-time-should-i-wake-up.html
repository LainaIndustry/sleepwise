What Time Should I Wake Up?

📅 January 12, 2026 ✍️ SleepWise Editorial Team 📂 Sleep Schedule ⏱️ 7 min read

One of the most common questions people ask about sleep is: "What time should I wake up?" The answer depends on several factors, including your bedtime, sleep cycle duration, and daily schedule. The key is to wake up at the end of a sleep cycle to feel refreshed and alert.

This guide will help you find your optimal wake-up time and create a morning routine that sets you up for success.

Why Wake-Up Time Matters

Your wake-up time affects more than just when you start your day. It can impact:

  • Energy levels: Waking at the right time reduces grogginess
  • Productivity: Morning alertness affects daily performance
  • Mood: Quality sleep improves emotional regulation
  • Overall health: Consistent wake times support circadian health

Understanding Sleep Cycle Timing

The average sleep cycle lasts about 90 minutes. During this time, your brain moves through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM sleep. Waking up at the end of a cycle—when you're in light sleep—leaves you feeling refreshed.

If you wake up in the middle of a cycle, especially during deep sleep, you'll experience sleep inertia—that groggy, disoriented feeling that can last for 15-30 minutes or more.

How to Calculate Your Ideal Wake-Up Time

To find your ideal wake-up time, start with your bedtime and count forward in 90-minute cycles. Here's the formula:

  1. Start with your bedtime (the time you actually fall asleep)
  2. Add 15 minutes (average time to fall asleep)
  3. Add 90 minutes × number of cycles (typically 5-6)
  4. The result is your wake-up time

Example: If you fall asleep at 11:15 PM and want 5 cycles:

  • 11:15 PM + 15 min = 11:30 PM
  • 11:30 PM + (90 × 5) = 7:00 AM
  • Your wake-up time should be around 7:00 AM

Creating a Morning Routine

Once you've found your ideal wake-up time, a consistent morning routine can help reinforce your schedule:

1. Wake Up at the Same Time Every Day

Consistency is key. Even on weekends, try to wake up within 30-60 minutes of your weekday wake-up time to avoid disrupting your circadian rhythm.

2. Get Morning Sunlight

Expose yourself to bright light within the first hour of waking. This helps regulate your circadian rhythm and signals your body to wake up.

3. Don't Hit Snooze

Hitting snooze can disrupt your sleep cycle and cause sleep inertia. When your alarm goes off, get up and start your day.

4. Stay Hydrated

Drink water first thing in the morning. Sleep depletes your body's water stores, and hydration helps you feel more alert.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is waking up early better for you?

Neither early nor late wake-up times are inherently better. What matters most is consistency and getting enough sleep. However, many people find that early wake-up times align better with their circadian rhythm and daily obligations.

What is the healthiest time to wake up?

The healthiest time to wake up is when you feel naturally refreshed and ready to start your day. For most people, this tends to be between 6:00 AM and 8:00 AM, but it depends on your individual needs and schedule.

Can I change my natural wake-up time?

Yes, you can shift your wake-up time through consistent effort. It takes about 2-3 weeks to adjust your circadian rhythm. Start by moving your wake-up time by 15 minutes every few days until you reach your target.
