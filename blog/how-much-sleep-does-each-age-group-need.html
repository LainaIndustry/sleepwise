How Much Sleep Does Each Age Group Need?

📅 January 5, 2026 ✍️ SleepWise Editorial Team 📂 Family & Age ⏱️ 8 min read

Sleep needs change throughout our lives. From newborns who sleep most of the day to older adults who may experience lighter sleep, how much sleep each age group needs varies significantly. Understanding these changes can help you support healthy sleep at every stage of life.

This comprehensive guide provides evidence-based sleep recommendations for every age group, based on guidelines from the National Sleep Foundation and the CDC.

Newborns and Infants (0-11 months)

Sleep is crucial for brain development and physical growth in the first year of life.

  • Newborns (0-3 months): 14-17 hours per day
  • Infants (4-11 months): 12-16 hours per day

Key considerations:

  • Sleep is typically divided into multiple short periods
  • Circadian rhythm begins to develop around 2-3 months
  • Safe sleep practices are essential (back sleeping, firm mattress)
  • Feeding and sleep schedules are closely linked

Toddlers and Preschoolers (1-5 years)

During these years, sleep patterns become more consolidated, and naps may still be important.

  • Toddlers (1-2 years): 11-14 hours per day
  • Preschoolers (3-5 years): 10-13 hours per day

Key considerations:

  • Most children transition to one nap by age 2
  • Bedtime routines become increasingly important
  • Sleep regressions may occur during developmental milestones
  • Consistent sleep schedule is crucial

School-Age Children (6-12 years)

As children enter school, sleep needs remain high to support learning and development.

  • Recommended: 9-12 hours per day

Key considerations:

  • Early school start times can conflict with sleep needs
  • Screen time and extracurricular activities may affect sleep
  • Sleep affects academic performance and behavior
  • Maintain consistent bedtimes even on weekends

Teenagers (13-17 years)

Adolescence brings significant changes to sleep patterns due to hormonal shifts and social pressures.

  • Recommended: 8-10 hours per day

Key considerations:

  • Circadian rhythm shifts (natural tendency to stay up later)
  • Early school start times often conflict with biological needs
  • Social media and screen time can interfere with sleep
  • Sleep debt is common in this age group

📱 Teen sleep tip: Encourage screen-free time 30-60 minutes before bed to improve sleep quality.

Young Adults (18-25 years)

The transition to adulthood often brings irregular schedules and competing priorities.

  • Recommended: 7-9 hours per day

Key considerations:

  • Balancing work, study, and social life can affect sleep
  • Establishing healthy sleep habits now benefits long-term health
  • College students often experience significant sleep deprivation

Adults (26-64 years)

During midlife, sleep is essential for maintaining health and managing stress.

  • Recommended: 7-9 hours per day

Key considerations:

  • Work-life balance can impact sleep
  • Stress management becomes increasingly important
  • Hormonal changes (menopause, andropause) can affect sleep
  • Sleep disorders (apnea, insomnia) may become more prevalent

Older Adults (65+ years)

Sleep patterns change significantly in later life, though sleep remains vital for health.

  • Recommended: 7-8 hours per day

Key considerations:

  • Sleep often becomes lighter and more fragmented
  • May experience earlier bedtimes and wake times
  • Medical conditions and medications can affect sleep
  • Regular physical activity can improve sleep quality

Frequently Asked Questions

Do sleep needs decrease with age?

While total sleep time may decrease slightly, the need for restorative sleep remains. Older adults may need the same amount of sleep but may experience changes in sleep architecture that affect how rested they feel.

Why do teenagers need so much sleep?

The teen brain is still developing, and sleep is crucial for this process. Hormonal changes during adolescence also affect sleep patterns, making teens biologically inclined to stay up later and sleep longer.

Is it normal for sleep to change with age?

Yes, sleep patterns naturally change throughout life. These changes are normal, but significant disruptions or chronic sleep problems should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

Medical Disclaimer: This article is for educational purposes only. Individual sleep needs vary. If you have persistent sleep problems, please consult a qualified healthcare professional.
